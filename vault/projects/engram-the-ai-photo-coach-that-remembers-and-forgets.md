Try it, no login — ?judge=1 drops you into a seeded photographer's live memory. Track 1 · MemoryAgent. Inspiration I'm a photographer. Early on I had a mentor who knew how I saw — not just whether a frame was sharp, but what I kept repeating, what I was finally getting right, and what still needed work between sessions. Two photographers can stand in the same place and come away with completely different pictures; coaching only works when someone remembers you . Most photo AI I've tried is excellent for a single critique and then treats every upload like a first meeting. You re-introduce yourself, or you get advice that could belong to anyone. Photography schools and workshop cohorts feel the same gap at scale: many learners, one human mentor, no continuity when the instructor moves on. An engram is the trace an experience leaves behind in memory. That's the difference between a critic and a coach — someone who knows where you started, notices what you've mastered, and moves the goalposts as you grow. I built Iris (a Gemini/Google-ADK photography mentor) for an earlier hackathon and learned the critique isn't the hard part. The memory is, and I'd barely scratched it. Track 1's brief — efficient retrieval, timely forgetting , recall under context limits — named exactly the engine I wanted to build on Qwen. What it does Engram runs one loop, forever: Critique — upload a photo; qwen-vl-max scores five dimensions with glass-box reasoning and grounded photography principles, in a steady ~30 seconds. Remember — every critique writes memory: salience-scored facts, skill evidence, genre identity. The app tells you what it learned from this photo — a strength reinforced, a new signal, or a weak frame treated as an exception ("one photo doesn't rewrite your plan"). Focus — watched skills graduate after three consecutive above-bar sessions: celebrated on your timeline, then retired from active coaching and assignment targeting. Forgetting as promotion, not deletion. Adapt — the next critique and every Mentor reply are built only from what's still true about you. A contradicted fact (Canon → Sony) gets a superseded_by link and never coaches you again, but stays visible for audit. Camera EXIF is stored as portfolio metadata today; feeding it into supersession is roadmap. The two ways a memory stops being used: a skill clears only on a consecutive streak (one miss resets it), and a contradicted fact is superseded with a link, never deleted. Recall sees only what is still true. What that feels like in the product: Home is a memory, not a dashboard — a mentor-read hero ("You're a natural light landscape shooter — composition cleared, now sharpening technique"), genre memory threads with real score-growth captions, and your current focus. Mentor chat with receipts — streamed replies grounded in your history, each shipping a Memory Receipt : what was recalled, what's been retired, what the token budget dropped. The numbers (frozen, reproducible) One question makes the whole thesis concrete. The photographer switched from Canon to Sony in session 3. Ask the memory: "What camera gear do I use?" Engram (shipped): "…switched to a Sony mirrorless body." — Sony only. Never-forgets baseline: mentions both the Canon and the Sony. The retired fact leaks back, three sessions stale. Across 26 frozen scenario traces (one-session flips, twice-replaced fact chains, multi-hop questions, controls), measured against two baselines: config mean FAMA recall of still-true facts context tokens Engram (forgetting on) 1.00 100% 1.72× fewer recency-only (keep the 5 newest facts) 0.64 100% baseline never-forgets (full history) 0.64 100% baseline Recall is 100% in all three — the engine isn't trading recall for forgetting; it gets both, at 1.72× lower token cost. The result worth sitting with is that the two baselines tie at 0.64: keeping only the newest facts scores exactly the same as keeping everything, because recency can't tell that a newer fact invalidates an older one. Both baselines leak a retired fact into 18 of 26 answers; supersession-aware forgetting is the only config that fixes it. (On this freeze every trace holds ≤5 facts, so recency's budget never actually drops anything — disclosed, and the point rather than a flaw.) MongoDB Atlas — memory items, skills, portfolio. Deployment evidence: docs/ALIBABA_CLOUD_PROOF.md The reliability layer. Qwen's JSON mode guarantees valid JSON, not your JSON. Validation failures used to trigger a model-based repair chain that could burn 93 seconds; now a local salvage layer ( app/output_salvage.py ) fixes the known deviation patterns — near-miss enums, out-of-range scores, malformed nesting — deterministically in ~0.1ms, with the model chain as last resort. Photo analysis went from "sometimes times out" to a steady ~30s. Every non-obvious engineering call is an ADR in docs/architecture/ , with the alternatives I didn't take and why. Challenges I ran into Gemini enforces your schema; Qwen trusts your prompt. My first live critique failed validation with seven errors; the fix was an explicit JSON skeleton in the prompt plus a shape-repair retry. Best of the seven: the model returned genre: "still_life" , refusing my enum because the photo genuinely was a still life and my taxonomy lacked it. The model was right; it's in the enum now. The 93-second repair spiral. A perfect critique failed validation on ONE cosmetic enum ( "medium" vs "moderate" ), triggering model-based repair calls that burned 93s and 502'd. I benchmarked the alternatives ( qwen-vl-plus : 2.5× faster, unparseable JSON in 5 of 6 tries) and made repair local and deterministic instead. That failure mode hasn't recurred. Memory as metrics vs memory as behavior. Testers said the UI proved the system but didn't feel like a mentor — "Retired advice: 2" is a database talking. I rebuilt the surfaces to narrate instead, every line template-derived from real state transitions with no LLM in that path. Deploying around a stuck verification queue. Alibaba identity verification queued "3 business days" at project start, so I made storage pluggable (local disk → OSS in one env flip) and the wait cost nothing. PAYG ECS turned out not to be gated at all — I deployed to Singapore while verification was still in review. Accomplishments that I'm proud of A live MCP round trip a judge can click.
